## 1. Decision Intelligence as a discipline — definition and boundary vs. BI, analytics, and data science

Gartner's canonical glossary entry defines decision intelligence as "a practical discipline that advances decision making by explicitly understanding and engineering how decisions are made and how outcomes are evaluated, managed and improved via feedback" ([Gartner IT Glossary](https://www.gartner.com/en/information-technology/glossary/decision-intelligence)), and Gartner's Peer Insights market definition operationalises the discipline as software that supports, automates and augments decisions with mandatory collaborative decision modelling, execution and monitoring, listing business intelligence as only one *optional* component technique ([Gartner Peer Insights](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/decision-intelligence-platforms)) — i.e. DI is positioned as a superset discipline in which BI is an input, not a synonym. The foundational practitioner writing is Cassie Kozyrkov's, which frames DI as "a new academic discipline concerned with all aspects of selecting between options" that fuses applied data science, social science and managerial science ([Kozyrkov, 2019](https://medium.com/data-science/introduction-to-decision-intelligence-5d147ddab767)). Hype-cycle placement for DI itself is *not* confirmable from Gartner's own 2025 AI Hype Cycle pages, which name AI agents, AI-ready data, multimodal AI, AI TRiSM, ModelOps and AI-native software engineering but do not mention decision intelligence ([Gartner, 2025 article](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/hype-cycle-for-artificial-intelligence); [Gartner press release, 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-05-gartner-hype-cycle-identifies-top-ai-innovations-in-2025)) — so the paper should cite Gartner's 2022 Top Strategic Technology Trends designation via secondary reporting, or the finance hype-cycle quotation, and mark current placement "n.a." Academic treatment exists but is fragmented: the strongest peer-reviewed conceptualisation is Moser et al.'s four-element DI framework, while adjacent IS journals treat analytics-driven decision making without adopting the DI label.

- [Definition of Decision Intelligence — Gartner Information Technology Glossary](https://www.gartner.com/en/information-technology/glossary/decision-intelligence) — Gartner, n.a. Canonical Gartner definition of DI as an engineering-and-feedback discipline; page states no boundary vs. BI or data science.
- [Decision Intelligence Platforms Reviews and Ratings](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/decision-intelligence-platforms) — Gartner Peer Insights, n.a. Market definition requiring decision modelling, orchestration, evaluation, governance and audit; lists BI, ML, optimisation, graph, simulation and AI agents as optional composable techniques.
- [Introduction to Decision Intelligence](https://medium.com/data-science/introduction-to-decision-intelligence-5d147ddab767) — Cassie Kozyrkov, TDS Archive on Medium, 2019. Foundational definition: DI as a discipline of "turning information into better actions at any scale," uniting data science, social science and managerial science.
- [Google's got a chief decision scientist. Here's what she does](https://www.wired.com/story/google-chief-decision-scientist-cassie-kozyrkov/) — WIRED, 2019. Documents Kozyrkov's Google role and the claim that decision science "transcends traditional data science" by connecting it to behavioural science and human decision-making.
- [Digging into decision intelligence](https://towardsdatascience.com/decision-intelligence-a-preliminary-summary-2098a9b79b46/) — Towards Data Science, n.a. Maps DI's constituent methods (analytics, frequentist/Bayesian statistics, ML, decision science, behavioural economics) and explicitly omits "data science" as a professional category rather than a method — a usable boundary argument.
- [Decision Intelligence: Creating a Fit between Intelligence Requirements and Intelligence Processing Capacities](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/22779752211017386) — Moser, Rengarajan & Narayanamurthy, IIM Kozhikode Society & Management Review, 2021. Peer-reviewed DI framework with four elements: Decision Context, Framework Proficiency, Intelligence Access, Decision Proficiency.
- [Decision Intelligence: A Critical Reflection](https://ideas.repec.org/a/for/ijafaa/y2024i74p42-44.html) — Roger Moser, Foresight: The International Journal of Applied Forecasting (International Institute of Forecasters), 2024. Short critical reflection on applying DI's data-to-action process.
- [Data Analytics Contributes to Better Decision-Making Beyond Organizational Boundaries](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misqe/vol19/iss2/5/) — Du, Pan, Xie & Xiao, MIS Quarterly Executive, 2020. ## 3. APQC Process Classification Framework (PCF)

The current cross-industry PCF is **version 7.4**, confirmed both on APQC's FAQ and on the PCF 7.4 definitions collection ([APQC PCF FAQs](https://www.apqc.org/process-frameworks/pcf-faqs); [PCF Version 7.4 Process Definitions and Key Measures Collection](https://www.apqc.org/resource-library/resource-collection/pcf-version-74-process-definitions-and-key-measures-collection)). Structurally, the PCF is a hierarchical taxonomy of 13 high-level Categories decomposing into Process Group, Process, Activity and Task, where each element carries both a hierarchy number and a **unique five-digit element ID that persists even when names and definitions change** ([APQC Introduction to the PCF, 2018](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/files/PCF%20Collateral/Intro%20to%20PCF%20-%20FINAL.pdf); [APQC PCF FAQs](https://www.apqc.org/process-frameworks/pcf-faqs)) — this stable-ID property is precisely what makes the PCF usable as a program-wide numbering spine. Industry variants retain the core structure and reference numbers of the cross-industry PCF so cross-industry benchmarking survives; current published variants relevant here are Automotive (OEM) 7.2.2, Retail 7.2.1 (developed with subject-matter expertise and IP from Microsoft) and Banking 7.2.2, i.e. all three trail the cross-industry release. Documented tooling use as a spine exists in the SAP stack: APQC PCFs are integrated into SAP Signavio Process Manager and importable as a folder tree of linked value chains, BPMN diagrams and activities.

- [Process Frameworks](https://www.apqc.org/process-frameworks) — APQC, n.a. States the PCF was developed in 1992, is a taxonomy creating a common language, and lists the Cross-Industry PCF Excel 7.4.
- [APQC's Process Classification Framework® (PCF) Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.apqc.org/process-frameworks/pcf-faqs) — APQC, n.a. Confirms version 7.4; describes hierarchy IDs and the unique five-digit PCF element ID used for benchmarking across name/definition changes.
- [Introduction to APQC's Process Classification Framework®](https://www.apqc.org/sites/default/files/files/PCF%20Collateral/Intro%20to%20PCF%20-%20FINAL.pdf) — APQC, 2018. Canonical structural description: 13 Categories → Process Group → Process → Activity → Task; industry PCFs keep the core structure and reference numbers.
- [PCF Version 7.4 Process Definitions and Key Measures Collection](https://www.apqc.org/resource-library/resource-collection/pcf-version-74-process-definitions-and-key-measures-collection) — APQC, n.a. Definitions for all elements of PCF 7.4; positions the PCF as global terminology to align activities against.
- [Industry-Specific Process Classification Frameworks](https://www.apqc.org/process-frameworks/industry-specific-process-frameworks) — APQC, n.a. Landing page for industry variants; states links default to the most recent PDF version (version numbers not shown on the page).

## 6. Sovereign AI, client-side AI deployment, and DMZ-style AI boundaries

NVIDIA's canonical framing is national-capability sovereign AI — "a nation's capabilities to produce artificial intelligence using its own infrastructure, data, workforce and business networks," including locally trained sovereign foundation models ([NVIDIA, What Is Sovereign AI?](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-telco-ai-factories-mc/en-us-telco-ai-factories/what-is-sovereign-ai)) — with the enterprise corollary that AI factories can be built on premises, "especially valuable in regulated sectors such as government, finance and healthcare" ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/sovereign-ai-agents-factories/)) and the harder statement that all infrastructure must sit within national borders under robust governance ([NVIDIA technical guide](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/lp/industries/global-public-sector/sovereign-ai-technical-overview/)). On the regulatory side, the AI Act itself does not mandate data residency — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies extraterritorially to third-country providers/deployers whose output is used in the Union, with EU data-protection law continuing to apply ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng)) — so residency pressure comes from adjacent EU policy (the Cloud and AI Development Act's highly critical use cases on EU-based capacity) rather than from the AI Act ([EPRS briefing, 2025](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2025/779251/EPRS_BRI(2025)779251_EN.pdf)). ## 7. Program memory and post-implementation knowledge loss

The strongest quantified primary source is PMI: organisations most effective at knowledge transfer improve project outcomes by nearly 35%, are three times as likely to have a formal knowledge-transfer process (92% vs. 33%), and **one in three unsuccessful projects (34%) is adversely affected by untimely or inaccurate knowledge transfer** ([PMI, 2015](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/pulse/capture-value-knowledge-transfer.pdf)). ## 8. Human-in-the-loop / human-authorized decisions in enterprise AI

The regulatory floor is EU AI Act Article 14: high-risk systems must ship human-machine interface tooling enabling natural persons to understand capabilities and limits, monitor operation, detect anomalies, counter automation bias, disregard/override outputs and safely stop the system — with the Annex III point 1(a) case requiring separate verification by at least two competent, trained, authorised persons ([Article 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)). NIST's AI RMF 1.0 converts this into management practice: human-oversight processes must be defined, assessed and documented, roles in human-AI configurations differentiated, and configurations range from fully autonomous to fully manual ([NIST AI RMF 1.0, 2023](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf)). ISO/IEC 42001:2023 supplies the certifiable management-system wrapper for responsible development and use, though the ISO pages do not specify detailed HITL mechanics ([ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001)). The scholarship is consistently sceptical that "a human in the loop" is self-executing: Enqvist finds Article 14 under-specifies what overseers must examine, when, and who performs it, risking superficial involvement ([Enqvist, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17579961.2023.2245683)); a 2025 analysis warns against treating oversight as a standalone safeguard given cognitive limits and automation bias ([SSRN, 2025](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5147196)); and for finance specifically, Buckley et al. argue oversight must be anchored in personal responsibility regimes plus AI due diligence, explainability and review committees ([Sydney Law Review, 2021](http://www.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/journals/SydLawRw/2021/2.html)). Encouragingly for adoption-first design, a Management Science field experiment finds keeping the human advisor with final say does not degrade advice quality and increases client alignment with the advice ([Yang, Bauer, Li & Hinz, 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v72y2026i1p242-264.html)).

- [Article 14: Human Oversight | EU Artificial Intelligence Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/) — artificialintelligenceact.eu (AI Act text), n.a. Full obligations: interface tooling, anomaly detection, automation-bias awareness, override/stop, and the two-person verification rule for Annex III 1(a).
- [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (Artificial Intelligence Act)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng) — European Parliament and Council, Official Journal of the EU, 2024. Authoritative legal text and scope for citing Article 14 in the paper.
- [Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf) — NIST, U.S. Department of Commerce, 2023. Requires documented human-oversight processes, differentiated human-AI roles, monitoring and shutdown/intervention capability.
- [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) — NIST, 2023 (released 26 January 2023). Canonical landing page confirming voluntary status and release date.
- [ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI management systems](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) — ISO/IEC, 2023. Requirements for responsible development, provision and use of AI systems; detailed HITL requirements not stated on the page (n.a.).
- [AI management systems: What businesses need to know](https://www.iso.org/artificial-intelligence/ai-management-systems) — ISO, n.a. Positions ISO/IEC 42001 as the first AI management-system standard whose Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle ensures "the correct level of oversight."
- ["Human oversight" in the EU artificial intelligence act](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17579961.2023.2245683) — Lena Enqvist, Law, Innovation and Technology (Taylor & Francis), 2023. Finds Article 14 leaves what/when/who of oversight under-specified, creating a risk of superficial human involvement.
- [Human Oversight under Article 14 of the EU AI Act](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5147196) — SSRN working paper, 2025. Oversight limited by cognitive constraints and automation bias; must not be the sole safeguard.
- [Human-in-the-Loop Artificial Intelligence: A Systematic Review of Concepts, Methods, and Applications](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/28/4/377) — Lazaros, Vrahatis & Kotsiantis, Entropy (MDPI), 2026. Defines HITL as operational human impact on development/deployment/supervision/governance; effective oversight needs authority, information, time, expertise and override capability.
- [Regulating Artificial Intelligence in Finance: Putting the Human in the Loop](http://www.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/journals/SydLawRw/2021/2.html) — Buckley, Zetzsche, Arner & Tang, Sydney Law Review 43(1), 2021. Proposes AI due diligence, explainability and AI review committees plus personal senior-manager responsibility.
- [My Advisor, Her AI, and Me: Evidence from a Field Experiment on Human–AI Collaboration and Investment Decisions](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v72y2026i1p242-264.html) — Yang, Bauer, Li & Hinz, Management Science (INFORMS), 2026. Human-final-say configuration preserves advice quality and increases client alignment, especially for riskier decisions.
- [Responsible AI](https://www.sap.com/products/artificial-intelligence/ai-ethics.html) — SAP, n.a. SAP's own principles include "human oversight and determination," with mandatory AI Ethics impact assessment and risk classification per use case; locally hosted AI keeps data in-region.
- [AI Governance Toolkit](https://www.tmforum.org/resources/toolkit/ai-governance/) — TM Forum, n.a. (constituent assets dated 2018–2022). Sector toolkit for demonstrating control and governance of AI at scale to regulators and customers.

## 10. Client vs. systems-integrator AI tooling balance

This is the thinnest theme, as expected: no peer-reviewed treatment of the SI-owned vs. client-owned AI asset boundary in transformation programs was located (**n.a.**), and the analyst commentary that discusses AI-enabled SI delivery mostly does not address ownership at all — UpperEdge's SI-market review and Altman Solon's genAI-era SI piece both describe delivery-model change without stating an ownership boundary. The clearest published articulation of the boundary comes from IDC, which names "Service Provider Agentic AI Platforms" as proprietary platforms built, operated and maintained by services firms that may be client-facing, may replace processes in the client environment, and **persist after the engagement ends**, and advises leaders to set explicit IP and data-ownership terms ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/from-labor-arbitrage-to-platform-led-outcomes-how-agentic-ai-is-rewriting-the-it-services-playbook/)). Vendor positioning is converging on hybrid models where the client ends up owning the asset: Accenture AI Refinery lets companies build their own agents and customise models on their own proprietary data ([Accenture](https://www.accenture.com/us-en/services/ai-data/ai-refinery)), and IBM Enterprise Advantage explicitly gives clients the approach and capabilities to build "their own tailored internal AI platform" from IBM's internal delivery platform while keeping their existing models and infrastructure ([IBM, 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-01-19-ibm-launches-enterprise-advantage-service-to-help-businesses-scale-agentic-ai)). The legal literature is where the boundary is actually specified — Morgan Lewis and Lathrop GPM both treat post-termination rights to models, fine-tuned parameters and outputs as negotiated, not default.

- [How Agentic AI Is Rewriting the IT Services Playbook](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/from-labor-arbitrage-to-platform-led-outcomes-how-agentic-ai-is-rewriting-the-it-services-playbook/) — Dina Capelle, IDC, n.a. Defines Service Provider Agentic AI Platforms as provider-owned, potentially client-facing platforms that persist post-engagement; urges explicit IP/data ownership terms.

## Gaps and open questions

Framed as invitations to peer review; each is a place where the fetched literature is thin, undated, or internally inconsistent.

1. **No confirmable current hype-cycle placement for decision intelligence.** Gartner's 2025 AI Hype Cycle pages name AI agents, AI-ready data, multimodal AI and AI TRiSM but never mention DI ([Gartner article](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/hype-cycle-for-artificial-intelligence); [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-05-gartner-hype-cycle-identifies-top-ai-innovations-in-2025)). Any claim in the paper about DI's hype-cycle position must be sourced to a paywalled Gartner document or dropped.
2. **DI has a definition but no accepted theory or measurement instrument.** The two peer-reviewed frames located — a fit-based four-element framework ([Moser et al., 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/22779752211017386)) and a platform/market definition ([Gartner Peer Insights](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/decision-intelligence-platforms)) — do not share constructs, and the MISQE analytics literature does not use the DI label at all ([MISQE, 2020](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misqe/vol19/iss2/5/)). Reviewers should press on whether this paper's framework is a new construct or a re-badging.
3. **DT4DI is telco-scoped and case-study-light.** TM Forum's own DT4DI pages publish an ontology, reference architecture, maturity model and use-case repository but name no operators or case studies ([DT4DI toolkit](https://www.tmforum.org/toolkits/digital-twin-for-decision-intelligence-dt4di-toolkit/); [IG1310C](https://www.tmforum.org/resources/introductory-guide/dt4di-top-use-cases-v8-0-0-ig1310c/)). Transferring a CSP maturity model to SAP-centric ERP transformation is an untested move. Note also the citation correction: DT4DI is IG1307/IG1310, not IG1247 ([IG1247](https://www.tmforum.org/resources/how-to-guide/ig1247-data-governance-tools-panorama-v2-0-0/)).
4. **The APQC industry variants lag the cross-industry spine.** Cross-industry is at 7.4 while Automotive, Retail and Banking are at 7.2.x ([FAQs](https://www.apqc.org/process-frameworks/pcf-faqs); [Automotive 7.2.2](https://www.apqc.org/resource-library/resource-listing/apqc-process-classification-framework-pcf-automotive-oem-pdf-0); [Banking 7.2.2](https://www.apqc.org/resource-library/resource-listing/apqc-process-classification-framework-pcf-banking-pcf-pdf-1)). A traceability spine that mixes versions needs a stated reconciliation rule; APQC's stable five-digit element IDs help but do not solve version drift.
5. **No published evidence that PCF numbering has been used as an end-to-end program traceability spine.** The confirmed uses are benchmarking and BPM-tool import ([SAP Signavio](https://www.signavio.com/reference-models/apqc-framework/); [SAP Help](https://help.sap.com/docs/signavio-process-manager/user-guide/import-apqc-framework)). Requirement-to-test-to-cutover traceability keyed on PCF IDs appears to be novel — and therefore unvalidated.
6. **AI-in-delivery evidence is vendor-reported, not independently replicated.** The headline figures (up to 50% less requirements-creation time, up to 20% less user-story time) come from SAP's own release notes ([SAP News](https://news.sap.com/2026/01/sap-business-ai-release-highlights-q4-2025/)), and the academic test-generation results sit in low-tier venues ([IJLEMR, 2024](http://www.ijlemr.com/papers/volume9-issue10/6-IJLEMR-89005.pdf)). Peer-reviewed evaluation of AI-generated functional specifications and AI-assisted hypercare in SAP programs: n.a.
7. **"Sovereign AI" is used in two incompatible senses.** NVIDIA's canonical definition is national capability ([NVIDIA](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-telco-ai-factories-mc/en-us-telco-ai-factories/what-is-sovereign-ai)), whereas the enterprise pattern this paper needs is customer-boundary hosting, which the AI Act does not actually mandate ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng)) and whose economics break even only at high volume or under residency mandates ([Pan & Wang, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18101v1)). The paper should define its own term rather than inherit the ambiguity.
8. **Client-DMZ agent deployments are asserted more than documented.** The available primary architecture statements are vendor patterns — managed-boundary agent workspaces ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-to-govern-autonomous-agents-in-enterprise-ai-factories/)) and MCP-gateway authority bounds ([IBM, 2025](https://www-api.ibm.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:c8af1164-1b81-49f0-bf9d-bb4dc8da1e19/original/as/ibm-guide-to-architecting-secure-enterprise-ai-agents-with-mcp-techxchange-2025.pdf)) — with no named enterprise reference deployment (n.a.).
9. **Program-memory loss is well evidenced in aggregate but weakly evidenced for consultant roll-off specifically.** PMI quantifies knowledge-transfer effects on outcomes ([PMI, 2015](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/pulse/capture-value-knowledge-transfer.pdf)) and ERP theses report post-go-live knowledge vanishing ([Revia, 2007](http://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:4611/FULLTEXT01.pdf)), but the widely quoted Standish failure rates are contested at the methodological level ([Eveleens & Verhoef, 2010](https://www.cs.vu.nl/~x/the_rise_and_fall_of_the_chaos_report_figures.pdf)). Any use of CHAOS figures should be defended or replaced.
10. **The IKEA case is real but must be cited to the right entity.** The multi-year ERP-driven transformation is Inter IKEA Supply's, running FY25–FY27 ([Inter IKEA FY25](https://www.inter.ikea.com/-/media/interikea/igi/financial-reports/fy25-financial-reports/inter-ikea-holding-bv-annual-report-fy25-final.pdf?rev=10c81cc96c064789932e13ebf6229492&sc_lang=en)), while the EUR 3.4 billion FY24 capex including business transformation and the role reductions are Ingka's ([Ingka FY24](https://www.ingka.com/static/ingkagroup_annualsummaryandsustainabilityreport_fy24.pdf); [Ingka newsroom](https://www.ingka.com/newsroom/creating-a-simpler-more-resilient-ikea-for-the-future/)). No IKEA/Ingka primary source states a single "multi-billion digital transformation program" figure (n.a.) — the paper should not assert one.
11. **Adoption-first sequencing rests on practitioner surveys, not experiments.** The strongest numbers are Prosci's, Wharton's, EY's and ERP Today's ([Prosci, 2025](https://www.prosci.com/blog/why-ai-transformation-fails); [Wharton/GBK, 2025](https://ai.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-Wharton-GBK-AI-Adoption-Report_Full-Report.pdf); [EY, 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/11/ey-survey-reveals-companies-are-missing-out-on-up-to-40-percent-of-ai-productivity-gains-due-to-gaps-in-talent-strategy); [ERP Today](https://erp.today/how-ai-is-forcing-erp-vendors-to-rethink-the-human-side-of-transformation/)). None tests sequencing (train-then-build vs. build-then-train) causally; the one HITL field experiment found is in advisory, not ERP ([Management Science, 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v72y2026i1p242-264.html)).
12. **The SI/client AI-asset boundary has commentary but no research base, and one directly opposing claim.** IDC describes provider-owned platforms persisting after engagements ([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/from-labor-arbitrage-to-platform-led-outcomes-how-agentic-ai-is-rewriting-the-it-services-playbook/)) while McKinsey suggests ERP *vendors* could own the agentic layer above the toolchain ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/our-insights/the-end-of-erp-as-we-know-it-five-ways-ai-is-disrupting-erp)) — a three-way contest (vendor / SI / client) the paper must adjudicate rather than assume.
13. **Human oversight may not scale to program-volume decisions.** Both the legal analysis and the HITL review warn that oversight degrades under cognitive load and automation bias ([Enqvist, 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17579961.2023.2245683); [Entropy, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/28/4/377)). A framework that requires human authorisation of many AI-proposed program artefacts should specify sampling, escalation and evidence retention, not just an approval step.

## Suggested peer-review venues

Each URL below was fetched during this pass; scope/submission details are as stated on those pages.

- [MIS Quarterly Executive — Information for Authors](https://aisel.aisnet.org/misqe/policies.html) — AIS Journals / AIS eLibrary. Criterion is new insight for IS and business professionals engaged in IS decision-making; ≤7,500 words plus an Addendum, single-blind review by generally no more than two editorial-board reviewers. Best fit for the framework-plus-case paper.
- [HICSS — Authors](https://hicss.hawaii.edu/authors/) — Hawaii International Conference on System Sciences. Papers submitted to one minitrack within 11 tracks; original material only; strict double-blind review June–August. Good route for an early version with a minitrack on decision support or enterprise systems.
- [Call for Papers: IEEE Software](https://www.computer.org/digital-library/magazines/so/cfp-ieee-software) — IEEE Computer Society. Peer-reviewed magazine positioned between pure research and pure practice; explicitly wants articles on tools, process improvement, project management and on past projects' failures and successes.
- [Communications Practice Section Welcomes Submissions](https://cacm.acm.org/practice/communications-practice-section-welcomes-submissions/) — ACM. Practice track takes deep dives, emerging best practices, cautionary tales and postmortems, evaluated for practicality and rigour including production evidence; email the section editors first.
- [Overview for CACM (authors)](https://authors.acm.org/magazines/cacm) — ACM. Author-facing entry point confirming that submission requirements vary by CACM section; use with the Practice-section page above.
- [Journal of Enterprise Information Management](https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jeim) — Emerald Publishing. Publishes work of intellectual interest and commercial relevance to managers, consultants and academics in information-intense enterprises, and explicitly accepts senior-manager/consultant enterprise case experience; open to special-issue proposals.
- [TM Forum — Collaboration projects](https://www.tmforum.org/member-projects/collaboration) — TM Forum. Member-only collaboration projects producing standards, best practices and toolkits across three Missions (including AI and Data Innovation), contributing to Open Digital Architecture; contribution requires membership and an IPR form rather than article submission.
- [ISACA Journal Article Submission](https://www.isaca.org/resources/isaca-journal/submit-an-article) — ISACA. 2,000–3,000-word practice-oriented articles with references, double-blind review by two or more reviewers, up to eight weeks; note the requirement that the article be the author's own creation without AI assistance — material for a paper about AI-assisted authorship.
- [Journal of Decision Systems — scope and editorial board](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/tjds20/about-this-journal) — Taylor & Francis. Scope covers decision-making processes and decision support including analytics, BI and intelligent/augmented decision systems; double-anonymised peer review. Natural home for the decision-intelligence theory contribution.
- [Intelligent Decision Technologies — editors' note](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/18724981251316079) — Sage (formerly IOS Press). Forum for theoretical and applied research combining AI, IT-based decision support and systems engineering — a fit for the sovereign-reasoning architecture component.
- [MIT Sloan Management Review — Author Guidelines](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/authors/) — MIT Sloan School of Management. Publishes short essays, opinion pieces, full articles and research highlights (1,000–2,500 words preferred) — **but the page states that MIT Sloan School of Management will cease publication of MIT SMR later this year and is no longer accepting submissions**, so this venue should be treated as closed for this paper.
